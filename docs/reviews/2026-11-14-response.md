# Response to the whole-repository review (14 November 2026)

Thank you for the review: it found the only cause of undelivered cars in our current weeks, and it was right about most mechanisms. This note answers each point with what we checked, what we changed and what the change measured. "Our weeks" means the current mainline's own saved weeks, not the season 3 board weeks the review used.

## What the mainline is now

While the review was written, the mainline moved on. `main` now defaults to fee model 2, bundles of three, limit 35 (45 before noon) and a bundle patience of 120 minutes (`b24-limit35-patience120`). Against the limit 25 setting on the same 40 weeks (dev, rob-01..20, holdout), that is +€27,072 (+5.9%), lowest week 93.7% instead of 92.8%. `dispatch.live` now plays `main` by default, so finding 9 is closed.

## Method

Each repair is a switch, `--set repairs=<names joined by +>`, so it can be measured alone. Every comparison is the same 30 weeks (dev-01..10, rob-01..20) played with and without it, from the same code; a week differs by €2,000 or more from noise alone, so only paired totals are used. The claims were checked with `tracks/orch/verify_review.py` on 40 saved weeks of the mainline.

## Point by point

### 1 and 2. A repost with a window that ends this minute is refused, and the car is never posted again

**Agree; this was a real bug.** In 40 weeks, all 8 undelivered orders ended with a refused post "The pickup window of R… ended". Two of them were the case you describe (a window that ends in the minute of the repost, penalties €563 and €647). The other three in the dev and rob weeks were cars still open when the week ended (Wednesday of week 2), where the world refuses any post and nothing can help.

Repairs: `windows` (a window that ends this minute counts as ended, `Desk.cut`) and `orphans` (in `tick`, an undelivered car without a gig and without a post in the outbox is posted again without windows, `Desk.post_bare`).

Measured (`b26-fix-wo`, 30 weeks): **+€45**, the same lowest weeks (93.7, 94.4, 94.8). Only two weeks changed. In rob-06 the rescued car gained €526. In dev-07 the rescued car arrived so late that its 100% refund and its fee cost €481 more than the undelivered penalty would have. So the bug was real and rare, and the repair is roughly neutral in money. **Adopted as the default** because a car that is never posted again is an error, and the final is one week.

### 3. Past `lastPickupAt` an open gig is dead until `gig_expired`

**Agree on the mechanism.** In our weeks, 95 gigs expired more than an hour after their order's last pickup minute, about 1,450 dead hours in all.

Repair `late`: one minute after `lastPickupAt`, an unbooked order's gigs are withdrawn and the car is posted again without windows (the desk wakes itself at that minute).

Measured (`b26-fix-wol` = windows + orphans + late): **−€1,837** against windows + orphans alone, and the lowest week fell from 93.7% to 92.4% (rob-17). Dead gigs after the last pickup minute fell from 70 to 15. Our reading: the reposted car is already urgent, so it goes on the urgent ladder (1.5 to 2.5 times typical) at once; earlier delivery saves a refund tier on some cars but pays the top fees on more of them. **Not adopted.** A variant that reposts at the fee the gig already shows, instead of the urgent ladder, is untested.

### 4. A dropped gig comes back at the top of its ladder

**Direction right, size smaller than claimed for the current code.** 1,221 drops in 40 weeks, 1,068 rebooked; the fee went from 0.96 to 1.29 times typical, and 477 of the 1,068 (45%, not 74 of 76) were rebooked at the top step.

Repair `drops`: on `gig_dropped`, a single-order gig that is not urgent is repriced to the fee it was booked at, then climbs again on a fresh ladder (`Desk.after_drop`).

Measured (`b27-drops`, 30 weeks): **+€2,441**, higher in 16 of 30 weeks, but the lowest week fell from 93.7% to 92.3% (dev-07 and rob-17). The fee of a rebooked drop fell only from about 1.29 to 1.25 times typical, and rebookings at the top step from about 45% to 35%: the second driver mostly takes the gig after it has climbed back. A gain of €80 a week with half the weeks on each side is inside the noise, and the lowest week got worse. **Not adopted** for now; it is the most promising of the repairs and worth a second look with more weeks.

### 5. Each `deadline_close` restarts the urgent ladder at 1.5

**Agree.** 158 of 527 warned orders got two or more warnings. You are also right that `fee_early` was measured together with this reset, so its result does not settle the idea.

Repair `keep_urgent`: a gig that already carries an urgent schedule is not repriced by a later warning. Measured (`b26-fix-all` = windows + orphans + late + keep_urgent): another **−€859** on top of `late`; orders warned twice fell from 120 to 66. Our reading: a second warning mostly follows a drop, and then the reopened gig keeps its old urgent schedule, which is at its top. The reset to 1.5 was, by accident, a cheaper restart. **Not adopted.** `fee_early` was not re-measured with `keep_urgent`.

### 6. The direct-offer study offered below any fee the gigs booked at

**Agree.** The offers were at 0.5 to 1.0 times typical, and the same gigs booked later at a median of about 1.35. The study cannot show whether offers at a realistic fee work. Not re-tested yet.

### 7. The intake counts every pending quote as a won order

**Agree on the code.** Switch `pending=<weight>`: a quote awaiting its dealer holds that share of a place.

Measured (`b26-pending04`, weight 0.4): **−€2,262**, lowest week 92.9% instead of 93.7%; with all the repairs together −€3,901. Counting pending quotes in full acts as a brake in request bursts, and with limit 35 that brake is worth keeping. **Not adopted.** The limit and the weight interact; we tuned the limit with weight 1.

### 8. The mainline sits to the right of the margin peak

**Consistent with what we found; not re-derived.** Raising the limit from 25 to 35, with bundle patience 120, added 9% more orders and €27,072 over 40 weeks. Going further hurt the lowest weeks: limit 40 −€2,213 with a lowest week of 90.8% (`b27-limit40`); limit 45 lowest 92.3%; a bundle patience of 180 minutes −€5,226, lowest 91.1%; limit 35 with `car=1.0` (lower prices) lowest **87.4%** (unranked). The leaders' 410 to 450 orders are not reachable with our current on-time control, in our measurements.

### 9. The final runs on hand-typed flags

**Agree; fixed** (see the top).

### 10. `invest` was never measured

**Not correct.** It was measured on 13 November, with the labels `b4-limit25-trust1-invest05`, `-invest1`, `-invest2`, `b4-trust1-invest1` and `b4-limit35-trust1-invest1` (10 dev weeks each). Every weight lost between €2,000 and €9,000 against the same setting without it, so it stays at 0. This was not written into a findings file, which is our fault and is why the review could not find it.

### Smaller points

- **Charging stop on short routes**: agree; RULES.md rule 3 asks for one only over 200 km. Repair `charging` is in the code and not yet measured.
- **Unused `Notes.risk`, `Facts.sure`, `Facts.summer_tyres`, forecasts**: agree; they will be pruned or documented before the jury.
- **A failed ranking run deletes the world; `harness.play` does not retry a timeout**: agree; not changed (no ranking run has failed so far).
- **`--label` reuses weeks after a code change**: agree; we name each change's label by hand, and that is easy to get wrong.

## Summary

| + late | −€1,837 | 92.4% | not adopted |
| + keep_urgent | −€859 more | 92.4% | not adopted |

The review's bugs were real, and fixing them did not add margin: the cases are rare, and the money they cost is mostly fees and refunds that a later rescue pays anyway. What moved the margin today was capacity: bundles and the limit.
